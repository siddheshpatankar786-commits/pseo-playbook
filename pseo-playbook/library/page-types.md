# Page type catalogue

Use this when choosing page types (stage 4) and writing page anatomy (stage 7). Each entry
gives the search intent, a URL and breadcrumb pattern, a starting section list, where the
uniqueness comes from, and the main risk. Adapt sections to the business; do not copy them
unchanged onto every site.

---

## 1. Industry solution page

- **Searches like:** "[product or service] for [industry]", "[industry] [problem] software"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/solutions/[industry]/` · Home › Solutions › [Industry]
- **Sections:** industry problem hero · 3 to 5 industry pain points · how it works in this
  industry (workflow) · case study proof · optional before/after table · FAQs · related
  industries
- **Uniqueness:** real case studies and workflows per industry, industry regulations,
  industry-specific terminology and constraints
- **Risk:** industries where the company has no proof. Build only where there is material.

## 2. Workflow or use-case page

- **Searches like:** "[task] software", "how to automate [workflow]", "[workflow] tool"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/use-cases/[workflow]/` · Home › Use cases › [Workflow]
- **Sections:** the job to be done · current manual way and its cost · step-by-step workflow
  in the product or service · screenshots or diagram · results or example · related use cases
- **Uniqueness:** the actual workflow steps, real screenshots, time or cost arithmetic from
  real data
- **Risk:** generic steps that would fit any tool.

## 3. Capability or AI use-case page

- **Searches like:** "AI for [task]", "[capability] for [function]"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/capabilities/[capability]/` or under the product
- **Sections:** what the capability does in plain words · where it fits in the workflow ·
  limits and requirements (data, setup) · example output · governance or accuracy notes ·
  related capabilities
- **Uniqueness:** honest limits, real examples, data requirements
- **Risk:** hype language and claims the product cannot back.

## 4. Technology hub and technology child pages (services firms)

- **Searches like:** "hire [technology] developers", "[technology] development services",
  "[technology] consulting"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** hub `/services/[service]/` with children; or keep existing root
  URLs and set the breadcrumb Home › Services › [Service] › [Technology]
- **Sections (child):** what teams use the technology for · typical project types · skills
  and seniority mix · engagement models · relevant case study · typical timeline · FAQs
- **Uniqueness:** case studies on that technology, delivery approach, real team composition
- **Risk:** this is the type most often duplicated (six or seven near-identical pages per
  technology). Consolidate to one strong page per technology plus genuinely distinct
  sub-topic pages.

## 5. Comparison page (X vs Y, alternatives)

- **Searches like:** "[A] vs [B]", "[competitor] alternatives"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/compare/[a]-vs-[b]/` · Home › Compare › [A] vs [B]
- **Sections:** quick verdict by situation · side-by-side table on the same criteria · where
  each is the better fit · pricing model notes · migration notes · FAQs
- **Uniqueness:** honest criteria, real feature data, the company's own experience
- **Risk:** one-sided comparisons; outdated competitor data. Date and review them often.

## 6. Integration or partner page

- **Searches like:** "[product] [tool] integration", "[platform] partner"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/integrations/[tool]/` or `/partners/[platform]/`
- **Sections:** what the integration or partnership does · setup or engagement steps ·
  example workflow · proof (a case study on that platform) · related integrations
- **Uniqueness:** real connector behavior, real platform-specific projects
- **Risk:** partner pages with no attributable work. Leave those out rather than invent proof.

## 7. Location page

- **Searches like:** "[service] in [city]", "[service] company [country]"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/locations/[city]/` · Home › Locations › [City]
- **Sections:** local office or team · local clients or projects · local regulations or
  market specifics · how engagements work there · contact for that location
- **Uniqueness:** real local presence, local projects, local rules
- **Risk:** doorway pages. Do not build pages for places with no real presence or service.

## 8. Case-study-led page

- **Searches like:** "[problem] case study", "[industry] [outcome] example"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/case-studies/[slug]/` · Home › Case studies › [Industry] › [Title]
- **Sections:** summary first (client type, problem, result) · constraints · approach by stage
  · key decisions · results with figures · what it would look like today
- **Uniqueness:** the case itself
- **Risk:** inventing missing details. Use only the case facts, arithmetic on them, and cited
  public context. Check NDAs.

## 9. Template, checklist or tool page

- **Searches like:** "[document] template", "[topic] checklist", "[metric] calculator"
- **URL / breadcrumb:** `/templates/[slug]/` or `/tools/[slug]/`
- **Sections:** the working tool or downloadable asset · how to use it · example filled in ·
  related templates
- **Uniqueness:** the asset itself. A working calculator or real template earns its place in a
  way text alone cannot.
- **Risk:** gated assets with nothing visible on the page.

## Page types to avoid or treat with care

- **Definition or glossary pages at scale:** AI answers now satisfy most "what is X" searches
  directly, so these pages are losing clicks. Build a few deep ones inside pillars instead.
- **Keyword-swap pages:** any type where only the keyword changes between pages.
