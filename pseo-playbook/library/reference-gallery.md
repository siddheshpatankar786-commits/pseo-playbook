# Reference gallery

Real programmatic page examples to put in developer and designer briefs. Each entry says
what to point out, and what a smaller company can realistically copy.

**How to use it:** pick two or three references per page type. Link the live page in the
brief, and write one line on what to look at. Live pages change, so open each reference
before sending and describe what is on it today rather than relying on old screenshots.
Never copy another site's text or visuals; references are for structure and ideas.

Traffic and page counts for these companies are widely quoted but vary a lot between
sources, so do not repeat specific numbers to a client unless you have checked a primary
source.

---

## Zapier: app directory and integration pages

- **Where to look:** zapier.com/apps, then any app's page, then an app-pair integration page
  (pattern `/apps/[app-a]/integrations/[app-b]`).
- **Show the developer:** the hierarchy. A directory links to app pages, each app page links
  to all of its integration pages, and each integration page links back up and across. It is
  the clearest public example of hub-and-child structure at scale.
- **Show the designer:** how a repeated layout still feels specific: real triggers and
  actions for that exact app pair, not generic copy.
- **What a smaller company can copy:** a hub page per service or technology that lists every
  child page with one line each, and child pages that link back to the hub and to siblings.

## Wise: currency converter pages

- **Where to look:** wise.com currency converter pages for any currency pair.
- **Show the developer:** each page carries a working tool (the converter), a chart of the
  rate over time, a short how-to, and links to other currency pairs. The data is live.
- **Show the designer:** the tool sits at the top. The page earns its place through what it
  does, not through paragraphs.
- **What a smaller company can copy:** one working component per page type: a cost estimator,
  a readiness checklist, a timeline calculator, a before/after table built from real case
  data.

## G2: comparison pages

- **Where to look:** any "[product A] vs [product B]" page on g2.com.
- **Show the developer:** comparisons generated from a structured database of products, with
  the same criteria for every pair.
- **Show the designer:** side-by-side layout, consistent criteria rows, a quick verdict near
  the top.
- **What a smaller company can copy:** a small set of honest comparisons on criteria the
  company can actually speak to, reviewed and dated.

## Canva, Notion, Airtable, Webflow: template galleries

- **Where to look:** each company's templates section.
- **Show the developer:** template pages grouped by category and use case, each with a preview,
  a short description and related templates.
- **Show the designer:** the asset preview is the hero; the text supports it.
- **What a smaller company can copy:** real checklists, templates or frameworks the company
  already uses with clients, published as individual pages within the relevant pillar.

## Talent and services marketplaces: "hire [technology] developers" pages

- **Where to look:** large developer marketplaces' hire pages (for example Toptal's
  "hire [technology] developers" pages).
- **Show the developer:** a consistent technology page type with skills, sample profiles or
  roles, and related technologies, all linked from a technology directory.
- **Show the designer:** proof and process near the top for a high-intent buyer.
- **What a smaller company can copy:** one strong page per technology, built on the company's
  own case studies and team composition, rather than several thin variants.

## Nomad List and similar data directories: city pages

- **Where to look:** nomadlist.com city pages.
- **Show the developer:** many data points per city (cost, internet speed, weather) from a
  structured dataset, so every page is genuinely different.
- **What a smaller company can copy:** the principle that a page needs several data points of
  its own, not one swapped word. For location pages, that means real local facts.
